How naming by nakshatra works

The Moon’s position at birth gives the birth star (nakshatra). Each of the 27 stars is split into four quarters called padas, and the old Avakahada table gives each pada a sound. Families use that sound as the first letter of the baby’s name. For example, Ashwini pada 1 is Chu, so names like Chudamani fit.

The naming ceremony (namakarana, peyar sootum vizha) is usually held on the 11th, 12th or 16th day after birth, or later on a good day.

Rare sounds and substitutes

A few padas have sounds that almost no names start with, such as ङ (nga), ञ (nya), ण (na), ठ (tha) and ढ (dha). Families use the nearest sound: na for ङ and ण, tha or ta for ठ, da for ढ, and gya or gnana (ज्ञ) for ञ. Tamil has no separate letters for these sounds, so Tamil lists use த for ட, and ந for ங and ண. Questions people ask

How do I find my baby’s nakshatra and pada?

You need the date, time and place of birth. Use the form above or make the free jathagam. The pada depends on the exact time, so a guess of the time can give the wrong letter.

Must the name start with the nakshatra letter?

No. It is a family tradition. Many families give the star-letter name at the ceremony and use another name in school, or use the letter only for the first syllable of a longer name.

Can I use a name from another pada of the same star?

Yes. When the pada sound is rare, picking a name from another pada of the same star, or from the letters of the baby’s rasi, is common practice.

Are these names only for Hindus?

The letters come from Indian astrology, but the names are Indian names used by many families. Every name here has a simple meaning so you can decide for yourself.

Choosing the first letter from the birth star is a tradition, not a rule. A name the family loves, that is easy to say and carries a good meaning, matters most.
