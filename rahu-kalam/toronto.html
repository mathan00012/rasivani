What is Rahu kalam?

Rahu kalam (also written Rahu kaal or Raahu kaalam) is a stretch of roughly 90 minutes every day that South Indian tradition treats as a poor time to start something new: a journey, a purchase, a business deal, an interview, a ceremony. Work that is already going on simply carries on. The custom is only about beginnings.

It is one eighth of the daytime, so it is shorter in winter and longer in summer, and it moves with your sunrise.

How is it calculated?

Take the time from sunrise to sunset at your place and split it into eight equal parts. Each weekday gives one of those parts to Rahu:

  • Monday: 2nd part
  • Saturday: 3rd part
  • Friday: 4th part
  • Wednesday: 5th part
  • Thursday: 6th part
  • Tuesday: 7th part
  • Sunday: 8th (last) part

The first part of the day is never Rahu kalam. An easy way to remember the order is "Mother Saw Father Wearing The Turban Suddenly" (Mon, Sat, Fri, Wed, Thu, Tue, Sun).

Printed tables that say "Monday 7:30 to 9:00" assume the Sun rises at exactly 6:00 and sets at 18:00. That is rarely true. Today sunrise is 6:07 AM in Chennai and 6:43 AM in New Delhi, so Rahu kalam there is 9:00 AM – 10:27 AM and 9:24 AM – 10:45 AM. Yamagandam, Gulika kalam and Abhijit muhurtam

Yamagandam (Yamakandam) and Gulika kalam (Kuligai) use the same eight-part day. Yamagandam is avoided for new starts just like Rahu kalam.

Gulika is the part of the day that belongs to Saturn. The classical text Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra describes this rule: divide the daytime into eight parts, give the first seven to the seven planets starting with the lord of the weekday, leave the eighth without a lord, and the part that falls to Saturn is Gulika's. Many families believe that whatever is done in Gulika kalam repeats, so they avoid it for funerals or taking a loan, and some prefer it for buying gold or property.

Abhijit muhurtam is the 8th of the 15 daytime muhurtas, the one around local noon. It is considered good for any start, except on Wednesdays.

What science says

Sunrise, sunset and the length of the day are astronomy. RasiVani computes them with the standard formulas in Jean Meeus' Astronomical Algorithms (the same method behind the NOAA solar calculator), good to about a minute for most places.

Whether a time of day is lucky is a matter of belief. No scientific study has shown that things started in Rahu kalam go wrong more often. We publish it because millions of families plan their day around it, and they deserve the correct time for their own town rather than a table made for 6 AM sunrise.

Where the custom comes from

Rahu kalam is a regional custom, strongest in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and it is printed in every South Indian panchangam. It is not found in the oldest Sanskrit texts, which speak of the eight-part day and of Gulika. In North India, people more often check Choghadiya and Rahu kaal together. On Tuesdays and Fridays many temples in Tamil Nadu hold a Rahu kala pooja for Goddess Durga during this very time, so for some devotees it is a time to pray, not only a time to avoid.

Questions people ask

Is Rahu kalam the same all over India?

No. It depends on sunrise and sunset, which change from place to place and day to day. Today Rahu kalam is 9:00 AM – 10:27 AM in Chennai and 9:24 AM – 10:45 AM in New Delhi.

Why is RasiVani's time different from my printed calendar?

Most printed tables assume sunrise at 6:00 AM, or use one city for the whole state. RasiVani uses your own town's sunrise and sunset for that exact date.

Is there Rahu kalam at night?

South Indian practice uses only the daytime Rahu kalam shown here. Some almanacs also divide the night into eight parts, but that is rarely followed.

Can I travel during Rahu kalam?

Tradition only says not to start a journey in Rahu kalam. If you are already travelling, nothing changes. It is a belief, so follow your family's custom.

What is the difference between Rahu kalam, Yamagandam and Gulika?

All three are one eighth of the daytime, each in a different part for each weekday. Rahu kalam and Yamagandam are avoided for new starts. Gulika is believed to make things repeat.
