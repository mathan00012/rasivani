One sky, two clocks

Every Indian calendar watches the same two things: the Sun moving through the 12 rasis, and the Moon growing from new to full and back.

Solar calendars (Tamil, Malayalam, Bengali, Odia, Assamese, Punjabi) start a month when the Sun enters a new rasi. That moment is the sankranti.

Lunar calendars (Telugu, Kannada, Marathi, Gujarati and the Hindi-speaking states) run a month from one new moon to the next (amanta, used in the South and West) or from one full moon to the next (purnimanta, used in the North). A lunar month is named after the sankranti that happens inside it: the month in which the Sun enters Mesha is Chaitra.

Why Pongal stays put and Diwali moves

Festivals tied to the Sun, like Pongal, Makara Sankranti, Puthandu, Vishu and Baisakhi, fall on almost the same Gregorian date every year (14 or 15 January, 14 or 15 April). Festivals tied to the Moon, like Ugadi, Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali, move about 11 days earlier each year.

The reason is arithmetic. A lunar month (new moon to new moon) is 29.53 days, so 12 of them make about 354 days. The solar year measured against the stars is 365.26 days. The gap of about 11 days adds up to a whole month roughly every 32 months, and the lunar calendar then adds an extra month, the adhika masa, which has no sankranti in it. In 2026 that was Adhika Jyeshtha (17 May to 15 June). The Surya Siddhanta already gives these month lengths and the rule for the extra month.

Same festival, many names

  • Sun enters Makara: Pongal (Tamil Nadu), Sankranti (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka), Uttarayan (Gujarat), Maghi (Punjab), Magh Bihu (Assam), Makaravilakku (Sabarimala).
  • Solar new year, Sun enters Mesha: Puthandu (Tamil Nadu), Vishu (Kerala), Baisakhi (Punjab), Pana Sankranti (Odisha), Pohela Boishakh (Bengal), Bohag Bihu (Assam).
  • Lunar new year, Chaitra Shukla Pratipada: Ugadi (Telugu and Kannada), Gudi Padwa (Maharashtra), Cheti Chand (Sindhi), Navreh (Kashmir), and the start of Chaitra Navratri and of the Vikram Samvat year in the North.
  • Other new years: the Malayalam year begins on Chingam 1, and the Gujarati new year the day after Diwali.

Amanta and purnimanta: same day, different month name

Both systems agree on every tithi. They only label the dark half (Krishna paksha) differently. Krishna Janmashtami is "Shravana Krishna Ashtami" in Telugu, Kannada and Marathi calendars but "Bhadrapada Krishna Ashtami" in Hindi calendars, and it is the same day. Festivals in the bright half carry the same month name everywhere.

Years and eras

  • Shalivahana Shaka (Telugu, Kannada, Marathi, and the national calendar): Gregorian year minus 78.
  • Vikram Samvat (North India, Gujarat, Nepal): Gregorian year plus 57.
  • Kollam era (Kerala): Gregorian year minus 824 from Chingam.
  • Kali Yuga year: Gregorian year plus 3101.
  • 60-year cycle (Prabhava to Akshaya): shared by the Tamil and Telugu-Kannada calendars. The Tamil year starts at Puthandu and the Telugu-Kannada year at Ugadi, but they carry the same name. 2026-27 is Parabhava in both.

The Indian National Calendar

India's official calendar was adopted on 22 March 1957 on the advice of the Calendar Reform Committee chaired by the physicist Meghnad Saha. Its year begins on 22 March (21 March in a leap year) as Chaitra 1, it counts the Saka era, and it is used in the Gazette of India and by All India Radio alongside the Gregorian date.

Questions people ask

Why does Tamil New Year fall on 14 April every year but Ugadi moves?

Tamil Puthandu follows the Sun (the day it enters Mesha), so it stays near 14 April. Ugadi follows the Moon (the day after the Chaitra new moon), so it moves between March and April.

Are the Telugu and Kannada calendars the same?

Yes. Both use the amanta lunar calendar (Chandramana) with Ugadi as new year, the same month names and the same 60 year names. Only some regional festivals differ.

Which calendar does Kerala use?

The Malayalam calendar, which is solar and counts the Kollam era. A month starts on the day of sankranti if it happens before about three fifths of the daytime is over, otherwise the next day.

What is adhika masa?

An extra lunar month with no sankranti in it, added about once every 32 months so that lunar festivals stay in their seasons. The next one after Adhika Jyeshtha 2026 comes in 2029.

What is the official calendar of India?

The Indian National Calendar (Saka calendar), used alongside the Gregorian calendar since 1957.
