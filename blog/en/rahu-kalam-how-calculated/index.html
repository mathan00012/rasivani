What Is Rahu Kalam? How It Is Calculated + Times for Every Weekday

What Rahu Kalam is and why it moves every day. The simple rule of splitting daylight into 8 parts, the Rahu Kalam time for each weekday, and why your local sunrise changes it.

The full story

Before starting something new, many people ask one question: is it Rahu Kalam now? What is Rahu Kalam, and how is it worked out? Let us keep it simple.

The eight-part rule

How is Rahu Kalam calculated? Take the daylight from sunrise to sunset, and split it into eight equal parts. If the day is twelve hours long, each part is ninety minutes. One of these eight parts is Rahu Kalam. Which one depends on the weekday, and that is why it moves every day.

Why Rahu?

Rahu is a shadow graha, linked to eclipses. So by tradition, people avoid starting new auspicious work in its time. This is a traditional belief, not a scientific finding. But crores of people still follow it.

Rahu Kalam by weekday

Which time on which day? Assume sunrise at six, and here is the order. Monday at seven thirty, Saturday at nine, Friday at ten thirty, Wednesday at noon, Thursday at one thirty, Tuesday at three, and Sunday at four thirty. But if your town's sunrise is different, the times shift too. So always check the exact time for your day. Rahu Kalam, Yamagandam and good times all depend on the sunrise.
