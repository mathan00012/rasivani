Rahu Kalam, Yamagandam and Gulikai: How They Are Calculated

Why Rahu kalam changes every day and city, how it is worked out from sunrise and sunset, and what people avoid during Rahu kalam, Yamagandam and Gulikai.

Published: 7 November 2026 · RasiVani

Three daily periods

Rahu kalam, Yamagandam and Gulikai kalam are three periods of about one and a half hours each that come every day. Rahu kalam is the best known and many people avoid starting important work in it.

How the time is worked out

The daytime from sunrise to sunset is divided into eight equal parts. Each weekday gives Rahu, Yama and Gulika one fixed part. For example, on Monday Rahu kalam is the second part, and on Sunday it is the eighth. Because sunrise changes with date and city, the clock time changes too.

Why your city matters

Chennai and Delhi can differ by many minutes, so a fixed table like 7:30 to 9:00 is only an approximation. Our panchangam calculates the exact sunrise for your town or pincode and shows all three periods for that place.

What people usually avoid

Families avoid starting journeys, new business, house warming, buying gold or vehicles and signing agreements during Rahu kalam. Work already in progress can continue. Gulikai is considered good for repeating things, so some avoid funerals in it.
