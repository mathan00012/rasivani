How a Palm Leaf Kept Knowledge Alive for 1,000 Years: India's Manuscript Secret

Before paper, India wrote medicine, maths and astronomy on palm leaves. How was a palm-leaf book made, with an iron stylus, soot and oil? How were insects kept away? Why recopying was the real secret, the scholars who saved Sangam poetry and Vemana's verses, and the Gyan Bharatam mission that has reported more than 1.2 crore manuscripts.

The full story

Before paper, our ancestors wrote on leaves. Medicine, maths, astronomy, music and poetry. All of it on palm leaves. How did a leaf keep knowledge alive for a thousand years? Let us find out.

How a palm-leaf book is made

First, the palm leaf is cut, boiled and dried in the sun. That keeps it flexible, so it bends instead of cracking. Then the letters are scratched into the leaf with an iron stylus. No ink, no pen. Soot mixed with oil is rubbed over the leaf. It settles into the scratches, and the letters appear in black. Finally, holes are punched, the leaves are tied with string and kept between two wooden boards. That is a book.

How they kept insects away

To stop insects, scented oils were applied to the leaves. The books were wrapped in cloth and kept away from damp. But the real secret is something else. A palm leaf lasts only a few hundred years. So every generation copied the old book onto fresh leaves. The leaf died. The knowledge lived.

The people who saved them

At one time, in some places, old manuscripts were floated down rivers, and many simply crumbled at home. In Tamil Nadu, U. Ve. Swaminatha Iyer, and in Andhra, C. P. Brown, went village by village collecting these leaves. Because of them, Sangam poetry and the verses of Vemana are still read today.

The Gyan Bharatam mission

Today, the Government of India's Gyan Bharatam mission has surveyed manuscripts across the country. More than one point two crore manuscripts have been reported, and they are now being saved in digital form. If your home or your temple has old palm leaves, do not throw them away. They are treasure.

What's inside them

Inside these leaves are medicine, mathematics and astronomy. Music, temple architecture and literature too. A whole civilisation's knowledge. On RasiVani, we will bring you their stories, one by one.
