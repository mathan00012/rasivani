Your Month Is Named After a Star: The 27 Nakshatras of Ancient India

Chaitra, Vaishakha, Kartika, Margashira... where do India's month names come from? The Moon's 27-star path, the full moon, the Pleiades, Karthigai Deepam and the number 108, in one short story.

The full story

Where month names come from

Chaitra. Vaishakha. Kartika. Margashira. We say these month names every year. But do you know where they come from? The answer is written in the night sky.

The 27 nakshatras

Watch the Moon for a month. Each night it shows up next to a different group of stars. It takes about 27.3 days to go once around the sky and come back to the same stars. So our ancestors divided its path into 27 parts, one house for each night. These are the 27 nakshatras. Ashwini, Bharani, Krittika, Rohini... all the way to Revati.

Nakshatras in the Vedas

Nakshatra names already appear in the Vedas, in texts like the Taittiriya Samhita. The Vedanga Jyotisha, credited to the sage Lagadha, is one of India's oldest texts on keeping time. A curious fact: the oldest Vedic lists begin with Krittika, not with Ashwini as we count today.

Full moons name the months

Now the secret. About once a month, the Moon becomes full. Each full moon happens near a particular nakshatra, and the month took that star's name. Full moon near Chitra: the month of Chaitra. Near Vishakha: Vaishakha. Near Krittika: Kartika. Near Pushya: Pausha. Near Magha: Magha. Near Phalguni: Phalguna. Tamil months now follow the Sun and Telugu months follow the Moon, yet both still carry these star names.

Krittika, the Pleiades

Krittika is the small, bright star cluster that astronomers call the Pleiades. On a clear, dark night, most people can see about six of its stars. In tradition, the six Krittika mothers raised the infant Murugan. That is why he is called Kartikeya. And in the month of Kartika, when the full moon meets Krittika, homes and hills glow with lamps: Karthigai Deepam.

27 × 4 = 108

One more number to notice. Each nakshatra has 4 quarters, called padas. 27 × 4 = 108, the same number of beads as in a japa mala. This is one of several explanations people give for why 108 is sacred.

Your birth star

The nakshatra the Moon was in when you were born is your birth star, your janma nakshatra. Families use it when choosing a name and when matching horoscopes for marriage.
