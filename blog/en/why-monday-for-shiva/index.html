Why Is Monday Shiva's Day? The Moon Secret + Karthika Somavaram

Why do millions fast for Shiva on Mondays? Monday is Somavara, the Moon's day, and Shiva wears the Moon. The Purana story of the waning Moon, why India and Europe share the same planet-named week, and what people do on Karthika Somavaram.

The full story

Every Monday, millions fast for Shiva. In the month of Karthika, even more. But why Monday? Why not another day?

The answer is in the name

Why is Monday Shiva's day? The answer is in the name. In Sanskrit, Monday is Somavara, the day of Soma, the Moon. And Shiva wears the crescent Moon on his head. That is why he is called Chandrashekhara, the one crowned with the Moon. The Moon's day became the day of the Moon's protector. That is why Monday belongs to Shiva.

Why the Moon wanes: a story

In the Purana story, the Moon married the twenty-seven daughters of Daksha, the twenty-seven nakshatras. But he loved only Rohini. Angry, Daksha cursed the Moon to waste away. Shiva gave him shelter, and so the Moon wanes and grows again.

One week, everywhere

Here is a surprise. The order of the week is the same in India and in Europe. Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn. Seven days, named for the same seven sky lights. Sunday is the Sun's day and Monday is the Moon's day. Two old worlds, one week.

What people do on Karthika Somavaram

On Karthika Somavaram, many bathe early and fast until evening. At dusk they light lamps and bathe the Shiva linga with milk and water. Anyone unwell need not fast.
