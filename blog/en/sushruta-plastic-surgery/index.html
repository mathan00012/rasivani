Plastic Surgery 2,000 Years Ago? Sushruta's Nose Surgery, Explained

The Sushruta Samhita describes rebuilding a nose step by step: a leaf to measure, skin from the cheek, two small tubes. How the 'Indian method' seen near Pune in 1794 reached London surgeons, and how Sushruta trained students on gourds and leather.

The full story

Do you think plastic surgery is a modern invention? An old Indian text describes how to rebuild a lost nose, step by step.

Rebuilding a nose

A nose surgery more than two thousand years old. The Sushruta Samhita says: first, measure the lost nose with a leaf. Cut skin of that size from the cheek, but keep one side attached, so blood keeps flowing to it. Turn it over the nose, stitch it, and place two small tubes to breathe. That same idea still sits at the heart of plastic surgery today.

How the world found out

In seventeen ninety-four, near Pune, an Indian practitioner rebuilt a man's nose with skin from his forehead. British witnesses wrote about it in a London magazine. Within twenty years, London surgeons were doing it too. It is still called the Indian method.

Sushruta's training school

Do you know how Sushruta trained his students? Not on patients, but on vegetables. Cuts on gourds and cucumbers, stitches on leather bags, needles on lotus stalks. He also describes more than a hundred instruments. Medical schools today do the same: practise on models first, people later. Sushruta said it first. One honest note: the Sushruta Samhita grew over several centuries, and scholars still debate its exact date.
