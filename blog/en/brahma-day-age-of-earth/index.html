One Day of Brahma = 4.32 Billion Years: That Close to the Age of the Earth?

How the Puranas count time: four yugas make a mahayuga, a thousand mahayugas make a kalpa, one day of Brahma, 4.32 billion years. The Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. How close that is, and where it does not match, compared honestly.

The full story

How old is the Earth? About four and a half billion years, says science. A number in our Puranas comes remarkably close. What is it?

One day of Brahma

How long is one day of Brahma? The Puranas build it step by step. The four yugas together make a mahayuga of four million three hundred and twenty thousand years. A thousand mahayugas make one kalpa: a single day of Brahma. That is four billion three hundred and twenty million years. For the creator, it is just one day.

The four yugas

The four yugas run in the ratio four, three, two, one. Kali yuga is the shortest: four hundred and thirty-two thousand years. Notice how four-three-two keeps returning, just like one hundred and eight. These were numbers our ancestors loved.

The honest comparison

Is Brahma's day really the age of the Earth? Here is the honest comparison. Science puts the Earth at about four point five four billion years. Brahma's day is four point three two. Remarkably close. But the universe is about thirteen point eight billion years old, and Brahma's whole life is far longer. So this is a striking match in scale, not a measurement. The astronomer Carl Sagan once pointed out that Hindu cosmology is unusual: its time scales are as vast as those of modern science.
