The Speed of Light, 700 Years Ago? Sayana's 2,202 Yojanas, Honestly Checked

In his commentary on Rigveda 1.50.4, the 14th-century scholar Sayana quotes a line: the Sun's light crosses 2,202 yojanas in half a nimesha. With one set of units that lands near the real speed of light. With others it does not. The striking match and the honest caveats.

The full story

Seven hundred years ago, an Indian scholar wrote down a number about sunlight. Many say it is the speed of light. Is it? Let us check, honestly.

Sayana's line

The fourteenth century, in the Vijayanagara empire. A scholar named Sayana wrote a commentary on the whole Rigveda. Explaining a hymn to the Sun, he quotes a line: salutations to you, who cross two thousand two hundred and two yojanas in half a nimesha. A yojana is a distance, and a nimesha is the blink of an eye. So this is a speed: the speed of the Sun's light.

Doing the maths

In some old reckonings, a yojana is about nine miles, and a nimesha is about a fifth of a second. Work it out with those values, and you get about one hundred and eighty-six thousand miles per second.

The honest check

Does a seven-hundred-year-old line really give the speed of light? Here is the honest answer. Light really travels about one hundred and eighty-six thousand miles per second. With one choice of units, Sayana's number lands very close. But old texts put the yojana anywhere from about four to nine miles. With other values, the number comes out at half. A striking match, not a proof. The real treasure is this: our scholars were asking how fast light travels at all.
