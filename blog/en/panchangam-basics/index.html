Panchangam Basics: Tithi, Vara, Nakshatra, Yoga and Karana

What the five parts of the panchangam mean, how they are calculated from the Sun and Moon, and how families use the daily panchangam.

Published: 12 November 2026 · RasiVani

Five limbs of the day

Panchangam means five limbs: tithi (lunar day), vara (weekday), nakshatra (the Moon’s star), yoga and karana. Together they describe the quality of a day in the Hindu calendar.

Tithi and karana

A tithi is each 12° the Moon gains on the Sun, so there are 30 tithis in a lunar month, 15 in the waxing (Shukla) half and 15 in the waning (Krishna) half. A karana is half a tithi.

Nakshatra and yoga

Nakshatra is where the Moon is among the 27 stars. Yoga comes from adding the positions of the Sun and Moon; there are 27 yogas such as Siddha and Shubha.

How it is used

Families check the panchangam for muhurtham, festivals and fasting days, and to avoid Rahu kalam. Our panchangam shows all five parts with sunrise, sunset and Rahu kalam for your own town.
