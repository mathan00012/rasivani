Your birth star (nakshatra), explained

Before any marriage talk, every Paati asks one question: "What is your star?" Here is what that star actually is.

  • There are 27 nakshatras, each 13°20′ of the sky (360° ÷ 27).
  • The Moon goes round the sky in about 27.3 days, so it spends about one day in each nakshatra.
  • Your birth star is the nakshatra the Moon was in at the moment you were born.
  • Each nakshatra has 4 padas (quarters): 27 × 4 = 108 padas, 9 in every rasi.

A sky cut into 27 slices

Ancient Indian astronomers divided the Moon's path into 27 equal parts, one for each night of its trip round the stars. Each part is a nakshatra (star or "lunar mansion"), named after a bright star or group in it: Ashwini, Bharani, Krittika (the Pleiades), Rohini (Aldebaran) and so on up to Revati. The Taittiriya Samhita of the Yajurveda already lists them with their deities.

The Moon takes about 27.3 days to go once round the sky against the stars (astronomers call it the sidereal month). That is why there are 27 nakshatras, and why the Moon moves into a new one roughly every day.

So what is "my star"?

Your janma nakshatra (birth star) is simply the nakshatra the Moon was in when you were born. Two babies born on the same day usually share it; babies born a day apart often do not. Because the Moon moves about 13° a day, the time of birth matters only when it was close to a boundary.

Families use the birth star to choose the first syllable of a name, to start the Vimshottari dasha, to match horoscopes (porutham) and to celebrate the star birthday each year.

Padas, and where 108 comes from

Each nakshatra is split into 4 padas of 3°20′. That gives 27 × 4 = 108 padas, and since each rasi is 30°, every rasi holds exactly 9 padas (12 × 9 = 108). This is one reason, not the only one, why 108 is a special number in Indian tradition, as in the 108 beads of a japa mala.

Questions people ask

How do I find my birth star?

Enter your date, time and place of birth in the free RasiVani birth chart. It shows your nakshatra and pada from the Moon's position.

Is the birth star the same as the rasi?

No. The rasi is the Moon's sign (one of 12, each 30°). The birth star is the Moon's nakshatra (one of 27, each 13°20′). Each rasi holds two and a quarter nakshatras.

Why do some calendars give a different star for my birthday?

If you were born when the Moon was near the edge of a nakshatra, a few minutes of birth time or a different ayanamsa can change the answer.
