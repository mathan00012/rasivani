Why 108? The maths behind the beads

Kutti counts the beads on Paati's mala and VANI loses count at 57. Why does a japa mala have 108 beads at all?

  • 27 nakshatras × 4 padas = 108.
  • 12 rasis × 9 padas = 108: every rasi holds exactly 9 nakshatra padas.
  • The Sun is about 107.5 of its own diameters from Earth, close to 108. A neat coincidence, not a design.
  • A japa mala has 108 beads plus one guru (meru) bead, which is not counted.

The sky adds up to 108

Indian astronomy cuts the zodiac two ways: into 12 rasis of 30° and into 27 nakshatras of 13°20′. Split each nakshatra into 4 padas of 3°20′ and you get 27 × 4 = 108 padas. Since 30° ÷ 3°20′ = 9, every rasi holds exactly 9 padas, so 12 × 9 = 108 as well. The same 108 small pieces make up both systems, which is why the navamsa chart (one ninth of a rasi) fits so neatly.

The Sun and the Moon

The Sun's average distance from Earth (about 149.6 million km) is about 107.5 times the Sun's diameter (about 1.39 million km). The Moon's distance is about 110 times its diameter. People love this, and it is a real measurement, but it is a coincidence of our solar system. Say "about 108", not "exactly".

Tradition

A japa mala has 108 beads for counting a mantra, with one extra guru bead that marks the start and is never crossed. Temples list 108 Divya Desams of Vishnu, and many hymns give 108 names (ashtottara shatanamavali). Tradition gives many reasons for 108; the sky arithmetic above is one of them, not the only one.

Questions people ask

Is 108 in the Vedas?

Large sacred numbers appear across Vedic texts, and 108 is very common in later tradition (108 names, 108 Divya Desams, 108 beads). The nakshatra pada count is the clearest astronomical link.

Why does a mala have 109 beads?

108 beads are counted; the extra guru or meru bead marks the start and is not counted.
